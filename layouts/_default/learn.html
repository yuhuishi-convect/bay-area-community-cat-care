A little preparation.
A better life outdoors.

TNR means trap, neuter, return. Community cats are humanely trapped, spayed or neutered, vaccinated, and ear-tipped, then returned to their original outdoor home after recovery.

Before you trap

  1. Know the cats and their location

    Count the cats and coordinate with nearby feeders. A friendly cat may be someone’s pet; ask local animal services about checking for an owner.

  2. Confirm a surgery appointment

    Check the clinic’s service area, cat requirements, total fees, and cancellation rules. Apply for any voucher in the order the program requires.

    Find surgery clinics →

  3. Arrange equipment and a helper

    Borrow suitable humane traps and learn how to use them. Plan transport and a safe recovery space before trapping. Volunteers may have waiting lists.

    Get local help →

  4. Follow the clinic’s care instructions

    Ask for written feeding, drop-off, recovery, and release instructions. Timing depends on the cat and procedure; ask the veterinary team about kittens or medical concerns.

Common questions

What does a tipped ear mean?

A small, flat cut at the ear tip identifies a cat who has already been spayed or neutered through TNR. Ask a local program before trapping an ear-tipped cat for routine surgery.

Where do cats go after surgery?

After veterinary-approved recovery, return community cats to the place they were trapped. Continue food, water, shelter, and monitoring. Ask an experienced group about special situations rather than relocating a cat yourself.

What if a cat is sick or injured?

Contact a veterinarian or local animal services promptly for guidance. Routine TNR appointments and volunteer request forms are not emergency services.
